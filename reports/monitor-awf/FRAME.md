# Frame — AWF market monitor

(DRAFT v1 — to be refined with the user; the monitor re-uploads this source
when it changes.)

We monitor the market around **English vocabulary, synonyms and word-choice
tools** to decide what **anotherwordfor.net** (an English synonyms site)
should build, write, and propose.

For every batch of market feedback (YouTube comments and transcripts, Reddit
threads, app-store reviews, articles), assess it against these tracked
questions:

1. **NEEDS — what is missing for users.** Example sentences and context for
   each synonym; nuance between near-synonyms (register, formality, intensity);
   collocations and prepositions; pronunciation; level tags (A1–C2 / IELTS
   band); offline and mobile access; speed; no ads/paywalls.
2. **WHAT WORKS — what users praise and why.** Which tools, sites, apps,
   methods (spaced repetition, comprehensible input, crowdsourced notes) earn
   love; what feature earns it.
3. **IDEAS — explicit wishes.** "I wish there was…", feature requests, formats
   (PDF export, quizzes, games, community lists).
4. **COMPLAINTS — what users hate in existing tools.** thesaurus.com,
   WordHippo, Power Thesaurus, QuillBot, Merriam-Webster, wordreference,
   dictionary apps, AI chatbots: ads, clutter, wrong suggestions, paywalls,
   broken features.
5. **SIGNALS — demand and distribution.** Search phrases people actually use
   ("another word for X…"), monetization remarks, where the audience gathers
   (channels, subreddits, apps), audience segments (IELTS/TOEFL takers,
   writers, ESL learners, native speakers).

Weighting principle: a point matters more when it appears across more
channels, under higher-view videos, with more comments under the item and a
higher comments/views ratio (engagement), and with more likes on the quoted
comments.
