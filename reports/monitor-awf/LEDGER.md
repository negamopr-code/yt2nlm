# Ledger — chronological novelty digests

## 2026-08-11a
- **T001** (complaint, NEW) Highly professional, original work written by hand is frequently falsely flagged as AI-generated, which is incredibly frustrating and unfairly forces researchers to run their own text through humanizing tools [1].
- **T002** (complaint, NEW) Advanced AI detectors like Winston AI are now highly effective at detecting automated "humanizers" and bypassers, defeating their main purpose [2].
- **T003** (complaint, NEW) Automated tools like JenniAI can provide highly irrelevant synonym suggestions, repetitive phrasing, and sometimes entirely fail to read or use uploaded PDF documents in the user's library [3].
- **T004** (works, NEW) Explaining synonyms, collocations, and contextual examples within actual essays is vastly superior to rote list cramming [4].
- **T005** (works, NEW) Learning vocabulary by topic and in whole phrases (collocations) is far more effective for tests like IELTS than studying isolated individual words [5].
- **T006** (complaint, NEW) Passively reading or being advised against taking notes is highly counterproductive; active note-taking and writing down words is crucial for long-term retention [6].
- **T007** (works, NEW) Active learning habits—such as underlining unknown words, listing their definitions, and rereading the original context later—drastically help expand vocabulary [7].
- **T008** (works, NEW) Utilizing monolingual (English-only) dictionaries, physically carrying pocket card decks, and grouping words by roots/synonyms/themes are highly successful strategies [8].
- **T009** (idea, NEW) Universities should replace lengthy essays with short answer questions or quizzes to test real comprehension and mitigate widespread AI-facilitated cheating [9].
- **T010** (idea, NEW) Onscreen vocabulary lessons are much easier to follow if all target words are listed together at the beginning of the video before the lesson starts [10].
- **T011** (idea, NEW) Visual learning of vocabulary can be significantly improved by underlining or highlighting target terms with distinct colors [11].
- **T012** (need, NEW) Writers require multiple alternative synonyms (at least 3) for key terms to prevent repetitive word choices in formal tasks [12].
- **T013** (need, NEW) Synonyms are rarely direct, 1-to-1 replacements; users need clear guidance on context, intensity, and register (e.g., the vast difference between "very skinny" and "skeletal") [13].
- **T014** (need, NEW) Students need reliable book recommendations or lookup tools to quickly find accurate synonyms when recording newly learned vocabulary [14].
- **T015** (works, NEW) Breaking down vocabulary study into small daily goals (e.g., 5 words) paired with daily quizzes builds a robust, long-term memory [15].
