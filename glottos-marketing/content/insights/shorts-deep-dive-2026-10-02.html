What the winners have in common

  • A "you're doing it wrong" hook. Both breakouts open with a correction (Stop saying…, Don't say…) and a red strike on a phrase the viewer actually uses. Marcus calls this a counter-narrative pattern interrupt. The "If you know these 5 words" quiz hook (L2) had its best result at 412.
  • An everyday word. "very big" and "I don't know" are things every learner says daily. "Meticulous" and "ubiquitous" are not, and the advanced lane lost 85% of viewers in the first seconds on Part 1.
  • Pictures. Only "Very Big" had a real illustration per word (a pumpkin for huge), and it is the top Short. "I Don't Know" did nearly as well without pictures, so pictures help but the hook matters more. All four very-X Shorts still to come have whiteboard pictures.
  • The burst shape. Both went from 0 to ~95% of their lifetime views within 6 hours (Very Big: 32 at +2.4 h, 810 at +5.5 h, then +4 per 3 h). That is one test pool passing, then the next gate failing on retention.

Why the others didn't work

  • Part 1: wrong background plus a hard word. It went live with the bug that flew "very big" synonyms behind every Short, and opened on "Meticulous" (stayed 15.1%). 97% of its views came from one country, so it never left its first seed pool.
  • Part 3 and Thank You: no test pool at all (1–2 views in 6–12 h, both verified public). I can't tell the cause from the data yet. Marcus's corpus points to two things: "Part N" titles read as missing context, and near-identical template posts every 6 hours look repetitive. Part 2, same template, got 412, so this is partly noise. Recheck at 48 h.
  • Long videos: 0–2 impressions. A dormant channel gets no browse traffic for long-form. The only long videos with durable traffic are the 2020–21 search titles ("Synonyms For Nice": 1,676 views, 70% YouTube search plus Google, 1:04 average view, +8 subs).
  • The old Words That Stick format (NLM podcast voice, 45–60 s): 29–33% stayed, 21–44 views. It's been replaced by the cards template.
  • Engagement CTA. "How many did you know? Write your score" got one comment ("3/5") across ~3,000 views. It has had no reply yet because the reply drafter is paused.

Diagnosis

  • Burst then stop = a small seed pool tested the Short and the next gate failed on stayed % and completion. Fix the first 1–2 seconds, not the topic.
  • Judge an angle on 10–20 posts, not 1–2 ("rule of 20"). Our 118 / 412 / 2 / 4 spread on one template is seed-pool noise.
  • "Part 3" in a title makes a scroller feel they missed something. Every Short must stand alone.

Do more of

  • "Duplicate what does well, forget what doesn't": 5–10 copies of the winner's exact structure within 24–48 h, changing the target word and the angle.
  • Shorter and fewer items: 8–15 s for one payoff, "avoid overload". 3 words, not 5 (our viewers leave at 16 s of 28).
  • Frame 1 does the work: the red X on the word you say, yellow on black, voice from 0.0 s, an open loop. A face with eye contact is his strongest lever, and we have none yet.
  • Hybrid titles: hook + the exact search phrase ("Stop Saying "Very Small"! Another Word for Small"), with the phrase also in description line 1.
  • Reply to every comment. Swap the score question for a debate or "which one is new to you?" question.
  • Title updates on flops; don't delete or re-upload the same file.

Build the funnel he always builds

  • Spoken short domain + pinned comment + Related video (his ranking for YouTube, which has no DMs) → a one-page "50 overused words" PDF on anotherwordfor.net → email (AWeber free tier) → 5-email drip linking to site pages.
  • Audience is global and non-native, so lead with free actions (download, free trial, email). Third-party offers first to learn what this audience pays for, then the Glottos app.
  • Same files on TikTok ("TikTok pushes new accounts") and Pinterest infographics pointing at the site.
  • No seeding to friends or WhatsApp groups: it confuses the algorithm about who the video is for.
