## Talking points (in this order)
1. HOOK (first words, one sentence): This is why you freeze mid-sentence.
2. You didn't forget the word. You're not sure it fits. Too formal? Too rude? Too dramatic?
3. So learn each word's setting, and when to avoid it.
4. Swamped is fine at work, but not when you only have one or two small tasks. Livid is too dramatic for someone who's just slightly annoyed.
5. "Let me look into that" suits emails and meetings, not chats with friends.
6. Five ways to say help, from casual to formal. The scene comes first and the words come last:
   - QUIZ 1 (a question, then a clear stop, then the answer): Friends moving a sofa: "Can you give me a hand?" Formal or casual? (stop, silence) Casual.
   - A neighbour carrying your heavy shopping: help. Fine anywhere.
   - The IT team fixing your laptop: that's support.
   - QUIZ 2 (a question, then a clear stop, then the answer): A hotel receptionist: "How may I assist you?" Formal or casual? (stop, silence) Formal. With friends, "assist" sounds stiff.
   - Lorries bringing food after a flood: that's aid. You hear it on the news, not in everyday chat.
7. THE RULE: Learn the setting, not just the meaning.
8. THE ACTION FOR TOMORROW: Tomorrow, next to each new word, write where it fits: friends, work, or both.
9. CTA: Look up "another word for help" on anotherwordfor.net. (Say the website as "another word for dot net".)
10. TEASER (last words): Next: the 15-minute routine.

## Delivery notes
- Point 1 is the first sound of the recording. Say it as a clear, confident statement.
- Point 2: the three short questions go UP, one after another.
- Point 6 has two live quizzes. Say the phrase, then ask "Formal or casual?" with the voice going UP, then a clear stop, then the one-word answer as its own new sentence, calm and clear. Never give the answer before or inside the question. The answer is the one word alone.
- Point 6: keep the five in this order: give me a hand, help, support, assist, aid. Scene first, word last, and stress the word a little.
- "Livid" is "LIV-id". "Receptionist" is "ri-SEP-shuh-nist". "Aid" sounds like "aid" in "first aid".
- About one minute in total. If you must shorten something, shorten the pauses between points, never drop a point.
