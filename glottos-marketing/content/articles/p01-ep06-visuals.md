### Flag decisions (settle `p01-ep06-format.md` §8)
**(a) Which two jobs are removed: "check if it fits" and "build the grammar". "Find the word" remains.** Article §7, verbatim: "The blank-mind moment happens when your brain tries to do three jobs at once: find the word, check if it fits, and build the grammar around it — while someone is waiting for you. The sentence method removes two of those jobs in advance. You practised the word in a full sentence, you know its setting, and you've already said it out loud. So in the real conversation, the word arrives with a ready-made sentence attached." Mapping: "you know its setting" -> *check if it fits* is done in advance (this is article §5 / Ep4's register rule: the doubt about fit is what costs the word). "You practised the word in a full sentence" + "already said it out loud" -> *build the grammar* is done in advance (the sentence frame already exists). *Find the word* cannot be done in advance: in the real conversation the word still has to come to you, and §7's last sentence says exactly that ("the word arrives", with the sentence attached). §7 lists three practice facts for two jobs because two of them (full sentence, said out loud) serve the same job; it does not say that finding the word is removed. So the format strategist's reading stands. The voice says it as "You know the word's setting, so you know it fits. You've already said it in a full sentence, so the grammar is ready." Panel 2 shows the same.
**(b) Hook = the verbatim article-intro quote** "I can understand 100% of the video, but when I try to speak, my mind goes blank." Read as a quote, no name, no "a learner said", no "in the comments". The episode map's paraphrase ("I understand everything… then my mind goes blank.") is NOT used anywhere. ⚠ Length: the quote is 17 words (~5 s at 0.9x), longer than the 4-s hook rule. Kept because the runbook and the Ep5 teaser require it; the quote card is on screen from frame 0 with "my mind goes blank" in the headline, so the visual hook lands in the first second (see §4 row 1).
**(c) choke and lost for words: DROPPED. clam up: also dropped.** *choke*: at B1 the first meaning is the physical one (food stuck in the throat), which clashes with the no-medical rule and makes a bad picture; the "fail under pressure" sense is informal, about sport or exams, not about speaking. *lost for words*: usually surprise or strong emotion, often good ("I was lost for words when they gave me the award"); an honest avoid-when note would cost ~4 s to say what it is NOT, and the picture (surprise party) contradicts the lesson. *clam up*: it means refusing to speak, often on purpose (to keep something private or out of shyness), and a blank mind is not a choice. Dropping all three also brings the spoken script to about 190 words, inside the ~1-minute / 75-s ceiling (8 scenes did not fit). The five kept phrases all describe failing to get words out while speaking.
