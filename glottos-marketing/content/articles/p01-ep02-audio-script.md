# AUDIO SCRIPT: Words That Stick, Episode 2 "Stop re-reading your word list" (brief Audio Overview, ONE presenter, under 60 seconds)

Instructions for the presenter. This is the COMPLETE script. Read it word for word, in order, as ONE presenter. Speak in short, clear, complete sentences for learners of English (level B1). Keep the whole recording UNDER 60 SECONDS. Your very first words are line 1: "Stop re-reading your word list." Your last words are line 15. Add nothing before line 1 and nothing after line 15.

## Do NOT say (hard rules)
- No introduction and no podcast language. Never say "this is the brief", "debrief", "welcome", "today", "today we", "in this episode", "let's dive in", "listeners", "you're listening", "podcast", "show", "episode", "host", "video", "deep dive", "word family", or any sign-off ("that's it", "thanks for listening", "see you next time", "until next time").
- No names, no universities, no years, no "a famous study", no "researchers found", no "scientists say". The only study fact is line 3, said exactly as written.
- No numbers except "sixty-one percent" and "forty" in line 3. Never say "percent of the words" or "percent of the vocabulary". Never say "four times", "seventy percent" or any other number.
- Never say that re-reading is useless, wrong or a waste of time. Never say "proven" or "science says".
- Never say "literally", "basically", "honestly", "super", "amazing", "game-changer", "hack" or "trick".
- Never say the answer word before the pause. In lines 5, 6 and 12, ask the question, STOP, then say the answer alone as its own sentence.
- Never say any fact, example, word or promise that is not written below. Never say that the website has pictures, example sentences, register notes, quizzes or an app.

## The script (15 lines, in this order)
1. Stop re-reading your word list.
2. Re-reading mostly trains you to recognise a word, not to find it when you speak.
3. A week later, people who practised recall remembered about sixty-one percent of what they studied. Re-readers remembered about forty.
4. So cover the answer, and make your brain find it. Try it now.
5. Very busy? (stop, silence) Swamped.
6. Very important? (stop, silence) Crucial.
7. That small moment of effort makes the memory stronger.
8. There are more ways to say very important.
9. The penalty kick that decides the match is crucial.
10. The air in a diver's tank is vital.
11. On a trip abroad, your passport is essential.
12. And a burst pipe that you must fix right now? (stop, silence) Urgent.
13. Tomorrow, cover the new words on your list. Read the meaning, say the word, then check.
14. For more words like these, look up "another word for important" on anotherwordfor.net. (Say the website as "another word for dot net".)
15. Next, you'll learn the best days to review a new word.

## Delivery notes
- Line 1 is the first sound of the recording. No greeting and no music bed talk before it.
- Lines 5, 6 and 12 are a live quiz. The question goes UP ("Very busy?"), then there is a clear stop, then the answer comes alone, calm and clear ("Swamped."). Do not add "the answer is" or "that's right".
- Lines 9 to 11: the scene comes first, and the word comes last in the sentence (line 11 starts with "On a trip abroad"). Stress the word a little.
- Pronounce "re-reading" as "ree-reading". "Percent" is "per-cent". "Vital" is "VY-tal".
- Total: under 60 seconds. If you must shorten something, shorten the pauses between lines, never the lines themselves.
