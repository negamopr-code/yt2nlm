# AUDIO SCRIPT: Words That Stick, Episode 3 "Review new words on these 3 days only" (brief Audio Overview, ONE presenter, about one minute)

Instructions for the presenter. These are TALKING POINTS, not a fixed text. Say every point below, in this order, in your own natural words, as ONE presenter. Speak in short, clear, complete sentences for learners of English (level B1). Keep the whole recording to ABOUT ONE MINUTE (never more than 75 seconds). Your very first words are the hook in point 1. Your last words are the teaser in point 12. Add nothing before point 1 and nothing after point 12.

## Do NOT say (hard rules)
- No introduction and no podcast language. Never say "this is the brief", "debrief", "welcome", "today we", "in this episode", "let's dive in", "let's break it down", "listeners", "you're listening", "podcast", "show", "episode", "host", "video", "deep dive", or any sign-off ("that's it", "thanks for listening", "see you next time", "until next time").
- No names, no universities, no years, no "a famous study", no "researchers found", no "scientists say", no "science says". Never say that this schedule is proven or scientific.
- No numbers except the days one, three and seven, and "five" in point 8. No percentages. The only days are day one, day three and day seven: no other day, week or month, and no other interval.
- Never name an app, a program or a website other than anotherwordfor.net. Never mention apps, flashcard programs or algorithms at all.
- Never say "review every day" as advice. The only time "every day" is allowed is in the rule "not every day, and not just once".
- Never call the five words in point 8 "synonyms" or "the same meaning". They are five ways to say strong, each for a different kind of thing.
- Never say that a memory or a word becomes "muscular", "mighty", "firm", "sturdy" or "durable". Those five words belong only to their pictures in point 8.
- Never say "search", "search for", "type in", "sites like" or "websites like". The website has no search box. Never say that the website has pictures, example sentences, register notes, quizzes, audio or an app.
- Never say "literally", "basically", "honestly", "super", "amazing", "game-changer", "hack", "trick" or "secret".
- Never add a fact, example, word or promise that is not written below.

## Talking points (in this order)
1. HOOK (first words, one sentence): Review new words on these three days only.
2. A word sticks best when you recall it just before you'd forget it.
3. Day one: see the word again, in its sentence.
4. Day three: cover it and recall it before you look.
5. QUIZ (a question, then a clear stop, then the answer): You know her face, but not her name. Recognise, or recall? (stop, silence) You recognise the face. You can't recall the name.
6. Day seven: use it in a new sentence of your own, out loud.
7. Each review resets the forgetting curve, and it falls more slowly each time. After day seven, a word you've used yourself is much harder to forget. The memory is strong.
8. Five ways to say strong, one scene each. The scene comes first and the word comes last:
   - A good handshake is firm.
   - A ladder that stays steady when you climb it is sturdy.
   - Hiking boots that last for years are durable.
   - A huge old oak tree is mighty.
   - A weightlifter's arms are muscular.
9. THE RULE: Days one, three and seven. Not every day, and not just once.
10. THE ACTION FOR TOMORROW: Tomorrow, see today's new words again, in their sentences.
11. CTA: Look up "another word for strong" on anotherwordfor.net. (Say the website as "another word for dot net".)
12. TEASER (last words): Next: why you freeze mid-sentence.

## Delivery notes
- Point 1 is the first sound of the recording. No greeting before it.
- Points 3, 4 and 6 each start with the day: "Day one", "Day three", "Day seven". Say the day, a short stop, then the step.
- Point 5 is a live quiz. The question goes UP ("recognise, or recall?"), then there is a clear stop, then the answer comes calm and clear. Do not add "the answer is" or "that's right".
- Point 8: say the scene first and the word last, and stress the word a little. Keep the five in this order: firm, sturdy, durable, mighty, muscular.
- "Recognise" is "REK-og-nize". "Durable" is "DYOOR-a-bl". "Mighty" is "MY-tee".
- About one minute in total. If you must shorten something, shorten the pauses between points, never drop a point.
