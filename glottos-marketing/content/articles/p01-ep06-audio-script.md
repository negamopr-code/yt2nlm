## Talking points (in this order)
1. HOOK (first words, read as a quote): "I can understand 100% of the video, but when I try to speak, my mind goes blank."
2. Sound familiar? Your memory isn't broken.
3. In that moment, your brain is doing three jobs at once: find the word, check if it fits, build the grammar. And someone is waiting.
4. Learning words in sentences does two of those jobs in advance. You know the word's setting, so you know it fits. You've already said it in a full sentence, so the grammar is ready.
5. So only one job is left: find the word. And it arrives with its sentence attached: "I was swamped at work this week."
6. What it feels like, in five pictures (scene first, phrase last):
   - A job interview, halfway through your answer: you freeze.
   - A pub quiz, an empty answer sheet: you draw a blank.
   - Talking to someone you like: you're tongue-tied.
   - A story at dinner that suddenly stops: you lose your train of thought.
   - A wedding speech: you stumble over your words.
7. You don't need a better memory. You need words that come with their sentences.
8. COMMENT: Comment the word you always forget.
9. WEBSITE: Find more words on anotherwordfor.net. (Say the website as "another word for dot net".)
10. CLOSING LINE (last words): That's the whole system. Watch the full series from Episode 1 in the "Words That Stick" playlist.

## Delivery notes

- Point 3: say the three jobs slowly, one by one, with a short stop between them: "find the word", "check if it fits", "build the grammar".
- Point 4: stress "in advance". Point 5: stress "one job".

- About one minute in total. If you must shorten something, shorten the pauses between points, never drop a point.
