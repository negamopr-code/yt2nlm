# AUDIO SCRIPT — Words That Stick, Episode 1: "Never learn a word alone" (brief Audio Overview, ~60–90 s)

Instructions for the two hosts: this is the COMPLETE script. Keep it short, warm and conversational, like two friends on a language podcast. Follow the beats in order, use ONLY the facts and examples written here, invent no statistics. End with the call to action.

## Beat 1 — Hook
You'll forget three out of four new words by next week. That isn't laziness; it's how memory works.

## Beat 2 — The forgetting curve
Hermann Ebbinghaus measured it in 1885: about 56% is gone after one hour, 66% after one day, 75% after six days.

## Beat 3 — The word in isolation
The problem is learning a word in isolation: livid = very angry. It's a label with nothing attached, so the brain files it as noise.

## Beat 4 — Give it a scene
Put it in a sentence instead: "He was livid when his flight was cancelled." A situation plus a feeling is a memory anchor.

## Beat 5 — The synonym spin (the heart of this episode)
Even better: don't learn "livid" alone, learn its family. Each synonym gets its own picture, so the words hold each other up:
- **cross**: mildly annoyed. A mum looking at muddy shoes on a clean carpet.
- **irate**: a customer arguing loudly at the service desk.
- **fuming**: stuck in traffic, steam practically coming out of your ears.
- **seething**: silent, jaw clenched, anger boiling underneath in a meeting.
- **furious**: slamming the door so hard the pictures shake.
- **livid**: pale with rage when the flight is cancelled.
- **incensed**: reading a deeply unfair headline and feeling it's an outrage.
- **apoplectic**: red-faced, so angry you can barely speak.
Line them up roughly from mild to extreme (cross → irate → fuming → seething → furious → livid → incensed → apoplectic) and you have a picture ladder. Be honest about it: cross is clearly the mildest and apoplectic the most extreme, but the words in the middle are close in strength and differ more in flavour: seething is anger you hide, irate is anger you complain with, incensed is anger at something unfair. Remember one picture and you pull up the whole family. That's what synonyms are for: nuance, not decoration.

## Beat 6 — Call to action
Never learn a word alone. Next time you look a word up, grab five of its synonyms and a picture for each. Find synonyms in seconds on anotherwordfor.net (say it as "another word for dot net"). Episode two: why re-reading your word list doesn't work.
