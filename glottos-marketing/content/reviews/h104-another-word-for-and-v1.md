In their words: "I repeat 'and' all the time and I don't know which linking word to use instead, or where to put it."
Solved = tomorrow they can take one "A and B and C" sentence they wrote and rewrite it correctly: "plus" when talking, a full stop + "Moreover," / "In addition," in an essay, and know which words are NOT a swap for "and".

What the video gives: the word list at 0:00, 5 speaking + 5 writing words with register and one example each, also/too position, plus vs essay, the moreover comma-splice fix (pays off the hook), "don't start every sentence with moreover", 4 two-choice quizzes, a shadowing block.
Gap: (1) no before/after: never shows an and-heavy sentence turned into a better one; (2) no warning that also/too/moreover/furthermore/additionally/besides are not conjunctions, so "and" cannot simply be replaced by them in "tea and coffee"; only plus / as well as / along with sit in the and-slot; (3) the most overused spoken "and" ("and then...", "and so...") is not touched.
