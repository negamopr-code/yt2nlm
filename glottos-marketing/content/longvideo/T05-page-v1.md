# Another word for hopeful: 35 better words (and other ways to say hope)

*Hopeful* is a good word, but there's more than one way to say it. Below are 35 alternatives, grouped the way people search for them:
for a hopeful **outcome**, a hopeful **outlook**, a hopeful **person**, a hopeful **future** and hopeful **thinking**, plus other ways to
say **hopefully**. Every word comes with its register (everyday, informal, formal) and an example. A few of them are traps: they sound
hopeful, but they're not compliments.

## What is another word for hopeful? {#what-is-another-word-for-hopeful}
The quick answer: **optimistic**. Four more everyday words:
| Word | Meaning | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| optimistic | expecting good things to happen | everyday, the most common | I'm optimistic about the interview. |
| positive | thinking about the good side of things | everyday | Try to stay positive. |
| upbeat | cheerful and hopeful | informal | She sounded upbeat on the phone. |
| confident | sure that something will go well | everyday — stronger than hopeful | We're confident we'll win. |
| encouraged | more hopeful because of something you saw or heard | everyday | We were encouraged by the first results. |

**Hopeful or confident?** *Hopeful* = you think it might happen. *Confident* = you're sure it will.
**Encouraged or encouraging?** The news is *encouraging*; you are *encouraged*.

## Another word for hopeful outcome {#hopeful-outcome}
When a result, a sign or a situation looks good:
- **Promising** — showing signs that it will be good. *The first results are promising.* (Not *promised*: that means someone said they would do it.)
- **Encouraging** — making you feel more hopeful. *That's very encouraging news.*
- **Favourable** (US *favorable*, formal) — good for you, or what you wanted. *We hope for a favourable outcome.*
- **A good sign** — something that shows things will go well. *He's eating again. That's a good sign.*
- **Looking up** (informal) — getting better. *New job, new flat. Things are looking up!*
In formal writing you can also say *a positive outcome* or *the desired outcome*.

## Another word for hopeful outlook {#hopeful-outlook}
Idioms for seeing the good side — and one trap:
- **Look on the bright side** — focus on the good part of a bad situation. *Look on the bright side: it was good practice.*
- **Glass half full** — seeing the good part first (the opposite: *glass half empty*). *She always sees the glass as half full.*
- **A silver lining** — a good thing that comes from a bad situation. *Every cloud has a silver lining.*
- **Light at the end of the tunnel** — a sign that a hard time is ending. *Two exams left. I can see the light at the end of the tunnel.*
- **Rose-tinted glasses** (US *rose-colored glasses*) — ⚠ not a compliment: seeing things as better than they really are. *He sees his school days through rose-tinted glasses.*
A joke that explains *outlook* better than a definition: the optimist says, "I can see the light at the end of the tunnel." The pessimist
says, "Yes. It's a train."

## Another word for hopeful person {#hopeful-person}
- **An optimist** (noun) — a person who expects good things to happen. *She's an optimist.* (Not *She's an optimistic*: *optimistic* is the adjective.) Opposite: *a pessimist*.
- **A ray of sunshine** — a cheerful person who makes others feel better. *Our new nurse is a ray of sunshine.*
- **Idealistic** — believing things can be much better than they are; sometimes not realistic. *He's young and idealistic.*
- **A dreamer** — someone who hopes for things that may not happen; praise or criticism. *Call me a dreamer, but I think we can win.*
- **Sanguine** (formal) — calmly optimistic, even when things look bad. *The manager was sanguine about the delays.*

## Another word for hopeful future {#hopeful-future}
- **A bright future** — a future that looks successful. *You have a bright future ahead of you.*
- **High hopes** — expecting something to be very good. *We have high hopes for this team.*
- **Look forward to** — feel happy about something that will happen. *I'm looking forward to seeing you.* (Here *to* is not part of the verb, so use the -ing form: not *to see you*.)
- **The sky's the limit** — there's no limit to what you can do. *With your talent, the sky's the limit.*
- **The world is your oyster** — you can go anywhere and do anything. *You've just finished school. The world is your oyster!*

## Another word for hopeful thinking {#hopeful-thinking}
Here hope meets reality. Positive thinking is a good thing; the others are about hopes that may not come true:
- **Positive thinking** — a good thing: choosing to think about good results. *Positive thinking helped her through the exams.*
- **Wishful thinking** — believing something good because you want it, not because it's likely. *A pay rise this year? That's wishful thinking.*
- **A long shot** — something with only a small chance of success. *It's a long shot, but let's try.*
- **A pipe dream** — a hope or plan that will almost certainly never happen. *A house by the sea? On my salary, that's a pipe dream.*
- **Hope against hope** — keep hoping when there is almost no chance. *We hoped against hope that the cat would come home.*

## Another word for hopefully {#hopefully}
Many people searching for *another word for hopeful* really need *hopefully*:
| Say this | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| with any luck | everyday | With any luck, we'll be home by six. |
| if all goes well | everyday | If all goes well, we'll open in May. |
| fingers crossed | informal | Big exam tomorrow. Fingers crossed! |
| here's hoping | informal | Here's hoping the rain stops. |
| I hope so / I hope not | everyday (short answer) | Will it be sunny tomorrow? I hope so. |
Starting a sentence with *hopefully* (*Hopefully, it won't rain*) is normal English today, even if some old style guides objected.
And it's always *I hope so*, never *I wish so*.

## Hope or wish? {#hope-or-wish}
The question learners ask most under hope and wish lessons. *Hope* and *wish* are not synonyms.
- **Hope = possible.** *I hope I can come.* · **Wish = not real, now or in the past.** *I wish I could come (but I can't).*
- **For the future, use hope + present:** *I hope you pass your exam.* (*I hope you'll pass* is fine too.) Not *I wish you will pass*; with *wish*, say *I wish you luck*.
- **After wish, go one step back:** *I wish I could speak French* (not *can*). *I wish I had more time.*
- **I wish I were / I wish I was:** both are used. *Were* is the textbook form — use it in exams and formal writing; *was* is common in speech and songs.
- **"We wish you a merry Christmas"** is a different use: *wish + person + thing* = wishing someone well (*I wish you luck*, *I wish you a happy birthday*). It doesn't mean it's unlikely.
- **Hope for the past** works when you don't know what happened yet: *I hope she got home safely.*

## Other ways to say "I hope …" in messages {#i-hope-phrases}
(Kept from the old page, rewritten to what people actually write.)
- **I hope this email finds you well** → *I hope you're well.* · *I hope you're doing well.* · *I hope all is well.* (formal: *I trust you are well.*)
- **I hope you feel better** → *Get well soon.* · *Feel better soon!* · *Wishing you a speedy recovery.*
- **I hope to see you soon** → *Looking forward to seeing you.* · *See you soon, I hope!* · *Can't wait to see you.*
- **I hope you have a good day** → *Have a great day!* · *Enjoy your day.*
- **I hope this helps** → *Hope that helps!* · *Let me know if you have any questions.*
- **Losing hope** → *losing heart*, *giving up hope*, *despairing* (formal, strong).
- **Hope to (do something)** → *aim to*, *plan to*, *aspire to* (formal, for big goals).

## Words to skip
Some lists of "another word for hope", including the old version of this page, give you *sanguineness*, *rosiness*, *buoyancy*,
*reverie* or *stock*. People rarely use them to talk about hope. *Fool's paradise*, *castles in the air*, *pipe dream* and *utopia* are about hopes that won't
come true — fine to use, but they're not compliments. And *greedy glutton* has nothing to do with hope.
