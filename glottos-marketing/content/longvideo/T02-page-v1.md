# Never give up synonym: 20 other ways to say "never give up"

"Never give up" is fine, but it gets old fast. Below are 20 other ways to say it, from what you text a friend to what you
write in a job application, each with its register and an example. One word that appears on many "never give up" lists is
actually criticism, not praise; it's in the "praise or insult" section.

## Say it to a friend (everyday English) {#say-it-to-a-friend}
- **Keep going** — continue, even when it's hard. *You're halfway there. Keep going!*
- **Hang in there** (informal) — don't give up while things are hard. *I know this week is awful. Hang in there.*
- **Keep at it** — keep working at something difficult. *Your English is getting better. Keep at it!* (But: *stick **with** it*, not *keep with it*.)
- **Stick with it** — don't stop, even when it gets boring or hard. *The first month is the hardest. Stick with it.*
- **You've got this** (very informal) — you can do it. *Big interview tomorrow? You've got this.*

Careful with *congratulations*: said to someone who has just failed, it sounds sarcastic. *Hang in there* is the kind choice.

## After a setback (idioms) {#after-a-setback}
- **Don't throw in the towel** — don't give up. In boxing, throwing in the towel means stopping the fight.
- **Push through** — keep going through something hard or tiring. *You can push through this.*
- **Bounce back** — recover quickly after a problem. *You'll bounce back from this.*
- **Pick yourself up** — get up again after a failure. *Pick yourself up and try again.*
- **Soldier on** — keep going, even when it's hard or unpleasant. *It's not easy, but we'll soldier on.*

## More formal words: for work, essays and job applications {#formal-words}
- **Persevere** (verb) / **perseverance** (noun) — keep trying for a long time, even when it's difficult. Pronunciation: it
  ends like *severe*, with the stress on the last part: per-se-VERE. Spelling: *persevere*, not *perserve*. Don't confuse it with
  *preserve* (keep something safe).
- **Determined** — having decided firmly to do something. *He was determined to finish the marathon.*
- **Persistent** — trying again and again, even when people say no. *She was persistent, and she got the job.*
- **Tenacious** — holding on and not giving up easily. *She's a tenacious reporter.*
- **Resilient** — able to recover after something bad happens. *Kids are often more resilient than we think.*

**Persevere or resilient?** *Persevere* is about keeping going. *Resilient* is about getting back up after something knocks you down.
And it's *I'm **not** resilient* (not + adjective), not *I'm no resilient* (no goes before a noun: *I'm no expert*).

## Praise or insult? Determined vs stubborn {#praise-or-insult}
Some words for "not giving up" are compliments; others are criticism.
- **Dogged** — praise: determined to continue, even when it's slow or hard (*dogged determination*).
- **Resolute** — formal praise: firm and determined.
- **Relentless** — praise for effort (*relentless training*), but also used for bad things (*relentless rain*, *relentless pressure*).
- **Stubborn** — usually criticism: refusing to change your mind.
- **Obstinate** — formal criticism: stubborn in an unreasonable way. It appears on many "never give up" lists, including the
  old version of this page, but you would never write it in a reference letter.

A little joke in English shows the difference: *I'm determined. You're stubborn. He's obstinate.* Same behaviour, three opinions.

## Words to skip {#words-to-skip}
- **Sedulous** and **pertinacious** — real words, but very rare and very formal.
- **Inexorable** — rarely used about people: it describes things that can't be stopped (*the inexorable rise of prices*).

## Common mistakes with "give up" {#give-up-mistakes}
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| When I feel give up… / When I feel giving up… | When I feel like giving up… |
| I gave up to drink coffee. | I gave up drinking coffee. |
| I won't give up you. | I won't give up **on** you. (give up **on** someone = stop believing in them; "give up your dreams" and "give up on your dreams" are both fine) |

## When not to say "never give up" {#when-not-to-say-it}
- **Rest, don't quit.** "Never give up" doesn't mean "never stop". A runner stops for water but doesn't leave the race. Taking
  a break, asking for help, or trying a different approach is part of not giving up.
- **Sometimes, don't say it at all.** If a friend is sad or exhausted, "never give up" can sound like pressure. Try *I'm here for you*.
- **Celebrate small wins.** *Well done, you're on the right track* keeps people going better than one big "never give up".

## What to say back when someone encourages you {#what-to-say-back}
*Thanks, I needed that.* · *That means a lot.* · *Thanks, I'll keep at it.*

## FAQ
**What's another word for never giving up?** *Perseverance* (noun) or *persevering* (adjective); in everyday English, *keep going* or *hang in there*.
**What is a formal synonym for never give up?** *Persevere* (verb), or adjectives such as *determined*, *tenacious* and *resilient*.
**Is "stubborn" a synonym for never give up?** Only as criticism. For praise, use *determined* or *dogged*.
**Other ways to say never give up to a friend?** *Hang in there, keep at it, stick with it, you've got this.*
