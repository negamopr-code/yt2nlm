## Beat 3 — Visual story: one scene, 15 words, mildest → strongest (0:50–4:30)

[Screen: one continuous whiteboard drawing that grows left → right; each word is written next to its picture as it is spoken.]

> Now the trick: pictures instead of a list. One story — just imagine every scene. It goes from calm to wild, and so do the words: from a little bit happy to extremely happy. Here we go.

### 1. content
[Hammock between two sleepy cows, cup of tea, breeze lines.]
> Content — say it con-TENT. You're lying in a hammock tied between two sleepy cows. A warm cup of tea, a soft breeze. You don't want anything else. That's content — quietly satisfied. You don't need more.

### 2. glad
[Dark cloud, one raindrop on nose, cloud drifting away, "Phew" bubble.]
> Glad. A dark cloud rolls over the hammock. One raindrop lands on your nose… and then the cloud drifts away. You breathe out: "Phew. I'm glad it didn't rain." Glad — happy about one particular thing, often with relief.

### 3. pleased
[Cow holding up a knitted scarf; you inspecting the stitches, nodding.]
> Pleased. One of the cows has knitted you a scarf. You hold it up, check every stitch, and nod: "Mm. Very nice work." You're pleased with it. Pleased — happy and satisfied with how something turned out.

### 4. chuffed [EXTRA]
[Paper plane looping round both cows, landing back in your hand; small proud grin.]
> Chuffed. You fold a paper plane. It flies a perfect circle around both cows and lands right back in your hand. You grin — you did that! You're chuffed. It's British and informal: pleased, often about something you did yourself.

### 5. cheerful
[Walking down a road, whistling notes, waving to people — and to a lamppost.]
> Cheerful. You jump out of the hammock and walk down the road whistling, waving "Good morning!" to everyone you meet — even to a lamppost. Cheerful — happy in a way that everyone can see and hear.

### 6. upbeat [EXTRA]
[Seagull flying off with your sandwich; you shrugging, thumbs up.]
> Upbeat. A seagull swoops down and steals your sandwich. You just shrug: "Never mind — I'll get an even better one!" Upbeat — positive and hopeful, even when something goes wrong.

### 7. joyful [EXTRA]
[Marching band of ducks with trumpets and drums; you marching behind, singing, music notes everywhere.]
> Joyful. Around the corner comes a marching band of ducks, playing a bright, joyful song. You join the parade and sing along at the top of your voice. Joyful — full of joy. We use it for music and celebrations, and it's more common in writing.

### 8. delighted
[Lead duck bowing, handing you a gift box with a ribbon; inside, a golden ticket.]
> Delighted. The leading duck stops, bows, and hands you a gift box with a red ribbon. Inside: a golden ticket. "Oh, how lovely — I'm delighted!" Delighted — very pleased, often about a gift, an invitation or good news.

### 9. thrilled
[Ticket reads "ROCKET — 5 MIN"; you jumping, punching the air.]
> Thrilled. You read the ticket: a seat on a rocket… leaving in five minutes! You jump up and down, punching the air. Thrilled — very happy and very excited at the same time.

### 10. over the moon
[Rocket arcing literally over the moon; phone screen "You passed your exam!"]
> Over the moon. The rocket blasts off and flies — yes — right over the moon. Your phone beeps: "You passed your exam!" You're over the moon. It's an informal idiom: extremely happy, usually about good news.

### 11. overjoyed
[Inside the cabin: happy tears floating as shiny bubbles, big smile.]
> Overjoyed. You're so happy you almost cry — and in space, your happy tears float around the cabin like little shiny bubbles. Overjoyed — extremely happy, usually about news or something that has happened.

### 12. on cloud nine [EXTRA]
[Big fluffy cloud with a painted "9"; you lying on it with a dreamy smile, little hearts/stars.]
> On cloud nine. The rocket drops you gently onto a big fluffy cloud with the number nine painted on it. You lie there with a dreamy smile, still thinking about the good news. On cloud nine — informal: extremely happy, as if you're floating on a cloud.

### 13. elated
[Cloud rising, arrows up; you with both arms raised: "I did it!"]
> Elated. Suddenly the cloud starts rising, higher and higher, and you throw both arms in the air: "I did it!" Elated — extremely happy and excited, usually after a success. You'll see it a lot in writing.

### 14. jubilant [EXTRA]
[Stadium below: cows, ducks, seagulls cheering, waving flags; you carried on their shoulders.]
> Jubilant. Far below, a stadium full of cows, ducks and seagulls is cheering your name and waving flags. They carry you round on their shoulders like a champion. Jubilant — very happy and celebrating, especially after a win. It's mostly written: you'll read about jubilant fans in the news.

### 15. ecstatic
[You dancing wildly, arms and legs everywhere, stadium shaking lines.]
> Ecstatic. And then the music starts. You dance like never before — arms, legs, everything. You can't stay still, and the whole stadium shakes. Ecstatic — so happy you can hardly contain it. The top of the ladder.

## Beat 5 — Reveal + ladder recap (4:50–6:15)

[Screen: "15, not 10!" — the 5 extras pop up highlighted. Then ONE screen: the ladder, bottom = content, top = ecstatic, each row = word · meaning · when to use.]

> And here's the sneaky part: I gave you fifteen words, not ten. Chuffed, upbeat, joyful, on cloud nine and jubilant were never on the list. Any of those you wrote down came from the story alone.

> Here's the whole ladder on one screen — roughly from a little bit happy to extremely happy. The order is a guide, not a law.

> Content — calm and satisfied. For a quiet, easy kind of happy.
> Glad — happy about one thing, often relief. Everyday speech: "I'm glad you came."
> Pleased — satisfied with a result. Neutral, fine at work: "We're pleased with the results."
> Chuffed — pleased, often with yourself. British and informal: with friends, not in a report.
> Cheerful — happy in a way people can see. For people, voices, even rooms.
> Upbeat — positive and hopeful. For moods, people and music.
> Joyful — full of joy. For songs and celebrations; more common in writing.
> Delighted — very pleased. Polite and safe in emails: "I'd be delighted to help."
> Thrilled — very happy and excited. Great in everyday speech.
> Over the moon — extremely happy, usually about good news. Informal.
> Overjoyed — extremely happy about news or an event. Neutral.
> On cloud nine — extremely happy, as if floating. Informal.
> Elated — extremely happy after a success. More common in writing.
> Jubilant — very happy and celebrating a win. Mostly written: news and books.
> Ecstatic — so happy you can hardly contain it. The top.

## Accuracy notes (for glottos-language-editor)

Meanings checked against standard learner-dictionary senses (Cambridge / Oxford Learner's). The acted scene shows the core sense; any extra flavour added by the scene is flagged "(scene flavour)".

| # | word | acted as | core meaning used | register / grammar note |
|---|------|----------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| 1 | content | lying in a hammock, wants nothing more | satisfied with your situation, not wanting more | neutral; mostly after a verb ("I'm content", "content with"). |
| 2 | glad | relief when the rain cloud leaves | pleased/happy about a particular thing; relief is a very common use ("glad it's over") | neutral/everyday; almost only after a verb — "I'm glad", not "a glad man". |
| 3 | pleased | inspecting a knitted scarf, nodding approval | happy and satisfied with something/someone ("pleased with") | neutral, slightly more formal than glad; common at work and in formulas ("Pleased to meet you"). |
| 4 | chuffed | paper plane does a perfect loop, proud grin | pleased (UK informal); "often about something you did yourself" = (scene flavour), a very common use ("chuffed with myself") but not part of the dictionary definition | British, informal; not for formal writing; many Americans don't use it. |
| 5 | cheerful | whistling, greeting everyone, even a lamppost | happy and positive, and showing it in your face/voice/manner | neutral; also for things ("a cheerful room", "cheerful colours"). |
| 6 | upbeat | shrugs off a stolen sandwich | positive, optimistic, cheerful; "even when something goes wrong" = (scene flavour) showing optimism | neutral-informal; for people, moods, speeches, music ("an upbeat song"). |
| 7 | joyful | singing in a duck parade | very happy; full of joy / causing joy | somewhat literary/written; common with songs, occasions, celebrations. |
| 8 | delighted | receives a gift box | very pleased | neutral to formal; very common in polite/business English ("We are delighted to announce", "I'd be delighted to"). |
| 9 | thrilled | jumps and punches the air at a rocket ticket | extremely pleased and excited | neutral, everyday speech ("thrilled to bits" is informal). |
| 10 | over the moon | rocket literally flies over the moon; exam news | extremely pleased and happy, typically about news/an event | informal idiom, especially British; widely understood. |
| 11 | overjoyed | happy tears floating in the cabin | extremely happy, usually at news/an event ("overjoyed at the news"); the almost-crying is (scene flavour) for intensity | neutral; mostly after a verb. |
| 12 | on cloud nine | lying dreamily on a cloud marked 9, still thinking about the news | extremely happy and excited (Cambridge); "as if you're floating on a cloud" = picture of the idiom, NOT a claim that it lasts a long time (gate 2026-09-27 removed "for a long time") | informal idiom. |
| 13 | elated | cloud rises, arms up, "I did it!" | extremely happy and excited, often because of something achieved | neutral-formal; more frequent in writing. |
| 14 | jubilant | stadium crowd cheering, carried on shoulders | feeling/expressing great happiness, especially because of a success/victory; not only crowds (an individual can be jubilant) — the crowd is just the picture; the recap says "after a win" | formal/written; typical in news ("jubilant fans"). |
| 15 | ecstatic | wild, uncontrollable dancing | extremely happy/excited; "can hardly contain it" = strength, not loss of control | neutral, also common in speech. |

Ladder caveat: the order is a teaching simplification. Items 10–15 are all "extremely happy" in dictionaries; they are ordered by how visible/energetic the image is. 5–7 (cheerful, upbeat, joyful) differ more in type (showing it / optimism / music-and-occasions) than in strength. The voice-over says "roughly" and "the order is a guide, not a law".

Items in the dialogue to double-check: "Very nice work." (pleased), "Never mind — I'll get an even better one!" (upbeat), "Oh, how lovely — I'm delighted!" (delighted), "You passed your exam!" (over the moon).
